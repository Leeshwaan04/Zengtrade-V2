01 honesty

It never fabricates a number.

Every terminal you have used will happily show you a stale price and let you believe it is live. This one will not. If the feed drops, if the position is unknown, if the value has not been computed yet, the field shows an em-dash. Not the last known value. Not a zero. Not a reasonable guess. A dash means: I do not know, and I will not pretend.

It is a small rule with large consequences. You can trust the screen precisely because the screen refuses to fill in blanks. And a system that will not lie to you about a price is not going to start lying to you about a P&L.

02 regime

The market has moods. The engine reads them.

Every cycle the engine takes live trend, India VIX, market breadth, momentum and persistence, scores them into a single composite, and names the regime, Bull, Neutral, Bear, Choppy or High-Vol. Nothing downstream is hardcoded. Position sizing, which strategy styles are allowed to fire at all, how much room the exits get: all of it retunes to the name on the board.

03 survival

Most strategies, most of the time, should do nothing.

This is the part that gets quietly removed from every other system, because standing aside does not demo well. A mean-reversion strategy in a strong trend is not a strategy to be optimised. It is a strategy that should stand aside until the tape comes back to it. The engine's default answer is no. A strategy has to argue its way past the regime first, and then past the Governor.

The open gap in the ensō is the whole point of the mark: the trade left untaken is still a decision, and usually the better one.

The Governor sits between every strategy and every order
concentration.symbol how much of the book a single name is allowed to become. —
concentration.sector the same ceiling, one level up, so a “diversified” book cannot quietly become one bet. —
crowding.max_bots_per_name how many strategies may crowd into the same symbol at once. —
exposure.total a hard ceiling across everything open, both books, at any moment. —
drawdown.kill_switch the level at which the engine stops trading, and stays stopped. —

the limits are yours and they live in your own config.
this page does not know them, so this page does not show them.

An engine that cannot say no is not risk-managed. It is just fast.

05 go-live

Nothing reaches real money on a click.

A strategy earns real capital or it does not get any. There is no button on any screen that promotes a strategy to live. Three gates stand in the way, they are checked inside the bot's own process, and all three must hold at the same time.

Gate 01

proof

A run of closed, profitable forward-tested trades. Closed, not open. Realised, not marked. Hope does not clear this gate.

Gate 02

funding

A funded account behind it. No phantom capital, no simulated balance standing in for money that does not exist.

Gate 03

arming

An explicit arming flag, set in the bot's own environment, by hand. The interface cannot set it. Neither can a strategy.

live and only then

∧ gate 01 and gate 02 and gate 03, never any two of three

Proof, not hope.

The interface can ask. Only the environment can allow. That asymmetry is deliberate: the worst trading day of your life should require you to have typed something, on purpose, the day before.
